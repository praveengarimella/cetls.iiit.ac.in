Briefing for the Chief Guest

On the launch of the Centre for Educational Technology and Learning Sciences (CETLS), International Institute of Information Technology, Hyderabad.

IIIT Hyderabad is establishing CETLS as a research centre on how people learn, and on how learning should be designed, from school through working life and into later years. We would like you to inaugurate the Centre.

The reason for a centre of this kind is not another set of classroom tools. Education systems are already under pressure from AI, and most of the response so far has been products: tutors, graders, content generators. Those can be useful. They do not answer the questions that actually decide whether a system improves. What is worth learning when machines can already do a great deal of the old work. How teaching should change. How people learn once technology is in the room. How we can tell, honestly, whether any of this has helped. Those are research questions. CETLS exists to work on them in Indian conditions, and to put the results into forms that others can use.

What we are trying to do is easy to say and hard to do. We want learning that is adaptive, engaging, and fair, and that is based on evidence rather than fashion. The work sits where the learning sciences, artificial intelligence, and educational technology meet. We will judge it by three tests: whether it is rigorous, whether it can be used at scale in India and the Global South, and whether it adds anything the field does not already know.

The same questions run through all of the Centre’s work, at every stage of life. In schools, that means the Indian classroom, multilingual settings, teacher capacity, and NEP 2020. In colleges and universities, teaching, learning, and assessment for a world already being rearranged by AI. For professionals and executives, continuous learning that fits work rather than the other way around. For older adults, learning that keeps people connected and participating, with dignity.

A good deal of serious work in the learning sciences never leaves the project that produced it. CETLS is therefore building an open technology layer, the Learning Middleware, so that teachers, faculty, institutions, and researchers can actually use those methods. It will be released as open source. This is not a platform for its own sake. It is a way to lower the cost of doing evidence-based work, and to make the Centre’s research usable beyond IIIT Hyderabad.

If you speak at the inauguration, three points may be useful. Learning is a lifespan matter; national capability does not end with a degree. AI is not merely a new set of tools; it changes what we teach, how we teach, how people learn, and how we can assess, and that is research. India can lead if we stay close to our schools, campuses, workplaces, and ageing communities, and if we share infrastructure instead of locking it up.

We ask you to inaugurate the Centre, and in doing so to mark that this is a serious, evidence-based agenda: that India means to contribute original research and open infrastructure, not only to adopt what is designed elsewhere.

The Centre is led by Prof. Vasudeva Varma, Professor at IIIT Hyderabad and Head of CETLS. Praveen Garimella is Associate Professor of Practice at the Centre. Prof. Sandeep Kumar Shukla, Director of IIIT Hyderabad, will receive you on behalf of the Institute.

The launch is on 5 September 2026 at IIIT Hyderabad.
